Business Loan Requirements: What Lenders May Review

Preparing for business finance starts with understanding the information a lender may use to assess the business, the requested amount and the ability to repay.

What may be requested?

There is no single checklist for every business loan. A lender may ask for documents that establish who owns the business, how it operates, how much it earns and what the requested funds are intended to do.

01
Business identity

Business registration or incorporation documents, ownership details, directors or partners, identification and contact information may be requested.

02
Financial records

Depending on the business and product, lenders may review financial statements, management accounts, tax records, invoices or other evidence of trading activity.

03
Bank statements & cash flow

Recent business bank statements may help demonstrate revenue patterns, operating cash flow, regular expenses and existing financial commitments.

04
Purpose of funding

You may be asked to explain how the funds will be used, such as working capital, equipment, expansion, inventory or another defined business purpose.

05
Repayment capacity

The lender may consider projected cash flow, existing debt, repayment obligations and other information relevant to the proposed facility.

What can improve preparation?

Keep records consistent

Make sure names, registration information, bank records and financial statements are consistent and current.

Know your numbers

Be ready to explain revenue, major costs, outstanding obligations and the reason you are requesting finance.

Prepare for additional questions

Some facilities may involve security, guarantees, collateral or additional evidence depending on the product and risk assessment.

Educational information: These are common considerations, not a promise of approval or a complete product checklist. Actual requirements, eligibility, pricing and documentation can vary by product, applicant, jurisdiction and current policy.
